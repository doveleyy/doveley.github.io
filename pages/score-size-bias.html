Bucket Size Drives Absolute Scores

Each point is a mapped TME bucket. The y-axis is the mean absolute bucket score across the GC PD-1 harmonised cohort. Larger buckets tend to have larger absolute scores, which is why bucket percentiles are a cleaner visual encoding than raw absolute contribution.

Takeaways
  • Genes per bucket correlate with mean absolute bucket score: r = 0.770.
  • Sub-buckets per bucket correlate even more strongly with mean absolute bucket score: r = 0.987.
  • Raw contribution mostly reflects bucket size, so percentiles are cleaner for patient-level interpretation.
  • PPI centrality weighting is part of the upstream score design: central genes, hubs, and bottlenecks can carry more weight before bucket and axis percentiles are derived.
